How to read this diagram

The barrier height φ_B0 is anchored to the metal's Fermi level and never moves — it's fixed by the metal and semiconductor alone (Schottky–Mott rule), independent of doping or bias. Everything that does change is on the semiconductor side:

  • Built-in potential, V_bi, is how much the bands must bend to align the bulk Fermi levels — it also depends on doping (how close E_F already sits to the band edge far from the junction).
  • Forward bias reduces the effective barrier the majority carriers see (V_bi − V_F), narrowing the depletion width.
  • Reverse bias adds to it (V_bi + V_R), widening the depletion region — this is what lets a Schottky diode block current in one direction.
  • If the doping and materials make V_bi ≤ 0, there's no barrier at all — carriers accumulate at the surface instead, and the "diode" behaves as an ohmic contact.
