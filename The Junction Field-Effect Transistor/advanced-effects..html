Channel-Length Modulation

1Channel Cross-Section — L, L′, ΔL

Beyond pinch-off the depletion regions meet before the drain, and the point of pinch-off moves toward the source as VDS rises. The remaining undepleted channel shortens from L to L′ = L − ΔL. Because current depends on the field over that shorter length, ID keeps creeping up with VDS instead of staying flat — this is channel-length modulation.

Velocity Saturation & Mobility

3Carrier Drift Velocity v(E)

At low field, carriers drift at v = μ0E — Ohm's law. As the field approaches the critical field Ec = vsat/μ0, scattering caps the drift velocity near vsat regardless of how much harder the field pushes. Short-channel GaAs devices routinely operate in this regime even at modest VDS.

4Constant vs. Field-Dependent Mobility

The dashed curve assumes mobility stays constant at every field — the textbook long-channel square law. The solid curve derates the same current by v(E)/(μ0E), showing how a real device's ID compresses and saturates earlier once the channel field approaches Ec.
Leakage & Subthreshold

5Subthreshold Current (log ID vs. VGS)

Below threshold the channel isn't fully off — a small diffusion current flows and falls off exponentially, so it plots as a straight line on a log axis. The swing S is the gate voltage needed to change ID by one decade; a smaller S means a sharper, more ideal turn-off.

6Gate Leakage Current (log IG vs. VGS)

A reverse-biased Schottky gate never blocks perfectly — a small leakage floor IG0 always flows. Push VGS toward forward bias and the gate diode turns on, and leakage rises by orders of magnitude over a fraction of a volt.
HEMT — Quantum Well & 2-DEG

7AlGaAs/GaAs Heterojunction — Simplified Quantum Well

Stacking doped n-AlGaAs on undoped GaAs creates a conduction-band discontinuity ΔEc. Donor electrons spill across the interface into GaAs, where band-bending traps them in a narrow triangular potential well right at the heterojunction — confined in one dimension, free to move in the other two. That sheet of confined charge is the two-dimensional electron gas (2-DEG). A more positive gate bias deepens the well and raises the sheet density ns; a sufficiently negative gate bias empties it.
